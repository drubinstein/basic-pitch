Streaming Basic Pitch

What we tried to get transcription out of Basic Pitch as fast as possible, what the numbers said, and why the demo works the way it does. November 2026.

The problem

The Basic Pitch model already runs window by window, so it can stream. The note creation step after it can't: it finds onset peaks across the file, and the melodia trick repeatedly takes the strongest leftover activation anywhere in the file. So notes only exist once the whole file is in. The goal was notes as the audio arrives, without retraining Basic Pitch.

What we tried

  • One runtime everywhere. Python and the browser both run the model with LiteRT (ai-edge-litert and LiteRT.js). Against the TensorFlow SavedModel, outputs differ by at most 1.5e-4, and no note changed on 14 test files. A custom Rust engine was considered and dropped in favour of a maintained runtime.
  • A streaming decoder. A small HMM per pitch (off, attack, sustain and gap states), decoded with online Viterbi. A note becomes final once every likely path agrees on it, or after 100 frames (1.16 s) at the latest. In JavaScript it takes about 15 µs per frame, about 2% of the model's time.
  • A fair baseline. The original note creation was tuned too (per dataset, on the dev halves). Tuned, it gains a lot: onset threshold 0.625, frame threshold 0.3, minimum length 7, energy tolerance 4, and the melodia trick off.
  • Tuning the HMM. Hill climbing gave all the gains. A PufferLib Protein sweep with latency as the cost never beat the starting parameters at the same latency. Per-register parameters didn't carry over from dev to test, and stronger re-strike handling made things worse.
  • A learned decoder. A small causal network on the model's note and onset outputs (per-pitch convolutions plus a 128-unit GRU across pitches), trained for about 30 minutes on a held-out split, with 0, 4 or 8 frames of look-ahead.
  • A ceiling check. The HMM tuned directly on the vocadito test tracks, to see how good any HMM tuning could get.

Why we went with what we did

  • The streaming HMM is the Live mode. It makes notes while the audio comes in, typically within a few frames of their start. It is far better than the original's defaults and close to the tuned original: ahead on vocadito, behind on MAESTRO Fno (.748 vs .774) and GuitarSet F (.600 vs .636, mostly re-strikes of ringing strings). Tuned per dataset, it pulls ahead on MAESTRO too.
  • It still uses its starting parameters. The tuned sets are per dataset, add about 100 ms, and gain 0.01 to 0.04 Fno. There is no single tuned set yet that's better everywhere.
  • No learned decoder. The spike lost to the tuned original on most tracks. Even the ceiling check, with the HMM tuned on the very tracks it's scored on, beats the tuned original on only 80% (Fno) and 85% (F) of vocadito tracks, short of the 95% we wanted. Different decoders reading the same model outputs end up at about the same score, so the remaining errors look like they're in the model's outputs, not the decoding.
  • Original mode stays for comparison. It is a line-by-line port of output_to_notes_polyphonic with predict()'s defaults, melodia trick included, and gives the same notes as the Python code on the test fixtures.
  • The site works like a map. Whatever is on screen is transcribed first. Each run of windows starts one window early so its notes match decoding from the start, the decoder keeps only 100 frames of history, and finished results are cached in the browser. On a 10-minute file, jumping around gave exactly the same 1,759 notes as going front to back, at about 97× real time on 4 threads.
